How it works (no printer needed)Read this first

1. What you need

  • A canvas, canvas board or thick paper (cardstock or watercolor paper). A hard, flat surface also works, like a wooden board painted white first.
  • A pencil, an eraser and a ruler
  • 2 or 3 brushes: a small pointy one for details and a wider flat one for big areas
  • A cup of water, paper towels, and an old plate to mix on
  • Your paints (tick them in My paints, or scan them from a photo)

2. Pick a project

Start with an Easy one. Each card shows if you can mix all its colors with your paints.

3. Draw the lines on your canvas

Pick the method that suits you:

A. Grid method (any size, easiest to get right)
  1. Open a project, switch to Numbers and turn on Show grid.
  2. Choose your canvas size. The page tells you how far apart to draw the lines, for example every 5 cm.
  3. With a ruler, draw the same grid lightly in pencil on your canvas.
  4. Copy one square at a time. Look at where each line enters and leaves the square, mark those points, then join them. Big shapes first, small details last.
  5. Erase the grid lines when you're done (or just paint over them).
B. Trace from your screen (small canvas or paper)
  1. Tap Tracing mode and turn your screen brightness all the way up.
  2. Lay thin paper (baking paper or normal printer paper) on the screen and trace the lines gently with a pencil. Don't press hard on the screen.
  3. Turn the paper over and scribble pencil all over the back of the lines.
  4. Put it on your canvas, scribbled side down, and trace the lines again. The pencil transfers onto the canvas.
  5. For a big canvas, zoom in and trace it in parts. Use the grid to line the parts up.
C. Window method (thick paper)

Tape your traced paper to a sunny window, tape your thick paper over it, and trace the lines you can see through it.

4. Write the numbers

Look at the Numbers view and write each number small and light in pencil inside its area. Tap a color in the list to see every place it goes.

5. Mix your colors

Each color shows a recipe made from your paints. Mix a bit more than you think you need, because mixing the exact same shade twice is hard. Cover the plate with plastic wrap so the paint doesn't dry out.

6. Paint

  • Paint big areas and the background first, small details last.
  • Paint light colors before dark ones.
  • Let each area dry 10 to 15 minutes before painting right next to it.
  • Wash and dab your brush dry between colors.
  • Paint looks patchy? Let it dry and add a second coat. Made a mistake? Let it dry and paint over it.

7. Finish

For cartoon characters, go over the outlines in black with your smallest brush. Let it dry overnight, then sign your name!

How to take a good photo
  1. Best way: put a small dab of each paint on a sheet of plain white paper. Leave a little space between them.
  2. Or open the pots and photograph the paint from straight above. Point at the paint itself, not the plastic.
  3. Use daylight near a window. Turn the flash off and avoid shadows.
  4. Get some white paper in the photo too. Tapping it fixes yellow or blue tints from your lighting.
  5. You can use several photos if your paints don't fit in one.

Your photos stay on your device. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

    Tip: the basic kit (white, black, red, yellow, blue) can make most colors. Adding a magenta or purple helps a lot for pinks, lavenders and purples; a phthalo blue or green helps for turquoise and teal.

    Recipes come from a paint-mixing simulation (pigments absorb light, so blue + yellow really does make green here). Real paints differ a little by brand, so always test a small amount first. “1 part” can be any equal measure: a drop, a spoon, a bottle cap.
